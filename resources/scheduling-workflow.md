# Social Media Scheduling Workflow

This workflow provides clear handoffs from idea to publication. Adapt the roles and gates to your team; a solo publisher can use the same checks as a personal checklist.

## Stages and ownership

| Stage | Action | Owner | Exit check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Plan | Set the audience, goal, channels, and cadence. | Planner | A brief exists for each planned post. |
| 2. Prepare | Draft copy and identify verified sources and media. | Creator | Claims and asset origins are recorded. |
| 3. Adapt | Tailor copy and media to each channel and format. | Creator/editor | Each version fits its intended placement. |
| 4. Review | Check facts, tone, rights, accessibility, disclosures, and sensitivity. | Reviewer | Approval is explicit and recorded. |
| 5. Schedule | Select the right account, format, time, and timezone. | Publisher | Calendar entry matches the approved version. |
| 6. Monitor | Verify publication and respond to failures or changed plans. | Publisher/on-call owner | Status and exceptions are recorded. |
| 7. Learn | Review outcomes in context and note improvements. | Planner/team | Observations inform the next plan. |

## Scheduling checklist

Before queuing a post:

- Confirm that the final copy is the approved version.
- Verify the target account, channel, and format.
- Check media selection, rights, crop, and accessibility details.
- Test links and confirm any required disclosures.
- Set the date and time with the intended timezone.
- Confirm what happens if the platform or connection rejects the post.
- Ensure someone owns the publication-status check.

## If AI is used

Treat output as an unapproved draft. Give the tool only appropriate context; check every factual assertion against a reliable source; remove unsupported claims; adapt tone and format; and have an accountable person approve the result. Do not automatically publish AI output solely because it passed a formatting check.

## Exceptions and pause plan

Decide in advance who can pause or cancel queued content if circumstances change. For a failed post, identify the error, determine whether retrying could create a duplicate, and record the resolution. For fast-changing or sensitive events, pause scheduled posts until a responsible person reviews them.
