# Choosing an AI Social Media Scheduler

The right scheduler is the one that supports your real channels and review process without adding unnecessary complexity. Compare tools against the same use cases rather than choosing by feature count or marketing claims.

## Step 1: Write down your requirements

List the social accounts, formats, team members, approval steps, timezone needs, and expected publishing volume. Include the tasks that are painful today: idea capture, versioning, review, scheduling, or checking whether a post actually published.

## Step 2: Use a consistent comparison framework

Score each criterion from **1 (does not meet the need)** to **5 (meets it well)** and mark unavailable or unverified information as **unknown**, not zero. Weight criteria based on your situation.

| Criterion | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Channel and format coverage | Does it support the specific account types and formats you use? Are there manual steps or restrictions? |
| Scheduling and reliability | Can you set the desired time and timezone? How are failures, edits, and cancellations surfaced? |
| Content calendar | Can you see drafts, scheduled items, and ownership in a useful view? |
| AI assistance | What can AI generate or recommend? Can every suggestion be reviewed and edited before scheduling? |
| Platform adaptation | Does it help tailor media and copy? Are constraints documented and current? |
| Collaboration | Are roles, comments, approvals, and client separation suitable for your team? |
| Analytics and exports | Can you access the data needed for your goals? Are definitions and retention clear? |
| Privacy and security | What permissions does the integration request? How are data, retention, and deletion explained? |
| Accessibility and usability | Can the team use the interface and review media and text accessibly? |
| Total cost and portability | What is included at your expected usage? Can you export your work or disconnect accounts? |

An example weighted score is `sum(score × weight) / sum(weights for scored criteria)`. Keep unknown criteria visible rather than treating missing information as a positive. A high score is a shortlist aid, not a guarantee of fit.

## Step 3: Test a representative workflow

Use a trial or demo to run one realistic post from brief through approval and scheduled status. Check a normal case and an exception: an edit after scheduling, a failed connection, or a changed publication time. Confirm exactly which steps are automatic and which require a person.

## Step 4: Validate claims and constraints

Verify publishing support against the platform's current official documentation. Check account eligibility, required permissions, formats, media constraints, and review requirements. Read the vendor's privacy and product documentation for claims about data handling and AI training; do not infer those policies from the presence of an AI feature.
