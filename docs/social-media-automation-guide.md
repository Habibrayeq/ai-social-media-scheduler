# Social Media Automation Guide

Social media automation uses software to handle repeatable tasks such as organizing drafts, routing approvals, scheduling posts, and reporting publication status. AI can assist with content tasks, but automation should have clear boundaries: routine work can move automatically, while consequential content decisions remain reviewable.

## A safe, practical workflow

1. **Define the outcome.** Choose the audience, purpose, channels, and a sustainable cadence.
2. **Create a brief.** Record the source facts, desired voice, audience, call to action, and constraints.
3. **Draft.** Write directly or use AI for ideas and variations. Mark generated text as an unapproved draft.
4. **Adapt per channel.** Check format, length, links, image/video requirements, and account eligibility using current platform guidance.
5. **Review.** Have an accountable person check accuracy, tone, accessibility, rights, disclosures, and sensitive topics.
6. **Schedule.** Set an explicit date, time, and timezone. Confirm which account and post format are selected.
7. **Monitor.** Check that the post published as intended. Handle failures, changed plans, and unexpected events.
8. **Learn.** Review outcomes against the original goal and use observations to inform—not blindly dictate—the next plan.

The [scheduling workflow checklist](../resources/scheduling-workflow.md) and [content planning template](../resources/content-planning-template.md) can be adapted to a team or solo workflow.

## Choose what to automate

Good candidates are repeatable, reversible, and easy to verify:

- Reminders for review deadlines.
- Draft status changes after an approval.
- Creating calendar entries from approved briefs.
- Notifications when a scheduled post fails.
- Collecting links to published posts for later review.

Use additional safeguards for actions that publish publicly, make factual or regulated claims, use customer information, or respond to people. Avoid unattended automation for sensitive or fast-changing topics.

## Practical example

For a weekly product education series, a manager records four verified tips in the planning template. An AI tool suggests two caption options per tip. The manager edits the drafts, checks every claim against the source, selects accessible media, and routes the posts to a reviewer. Once approved, the posts are scheduled with the target account's timezone. A team member checks publication status and logs any failures. The AI speeds up ideation, while a person owns final decisions.

## Operational safeguards

- Use role-based access and grant only the permissions the workflow needs.
- Keep credentials in the platform's approved connection flow, not in documents or scripts.
- Agree on who can approve, reschedule, or cancel a post.
- Maintain a way to pause queued posts during a crisis or major change.
- Record where source claims and assets came from.
- Recheck connected-account permissions and platform rules periodically.
- Never assume that a scheduled post will publish; build in status checks and a fallback.
